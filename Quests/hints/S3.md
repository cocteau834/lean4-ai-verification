## count_yes_yes

2. `decide`

## count_yes_no

## P_yes_yes
1. Unfold P, plug in the count, then do arithmetic.
2. `unfold P`, then `rw [count_yes_yes]`, then `norm_num`.

## P_yes_no
1. Same recipe with the other count.
2. `unfold P`, then `rw [count_yes_no]`, then `norm_num`.

## rr_is_private
1. There are 8 combinations of (out, truth, truth'). Split into cases and compute each one.
2. `intro out truth truth'`, then
     `cases out <;> cases truth <;> cases truth' <;>
        simp only [P, count_yes_yes, count_yes_no, count_no_yes, count_no_no] <;> norm_num`
     (`<;>` runs the next tactic on every case.)

## unbiased
1. On paper: 2(p·3/4 + (1 − p)·1/4) − 1/2 = p. Pure algebra.
2. `unfold estimate expectedYes` then `ring`.

## privacyFactor_eq
1. Clear denominators. field_simp needs to know that 1 − t ≠ 0.
2. `unfold privacyFactor`, then `have h : (1 : ℚ) - t ≠ 0 := (sub_pos.mpr ht).ne'`, then `field_simp`, then `ring`.

## more_truth_less_privacy
1. Use the formula you just proved, then compare the two fractions by cross-multiplying.
2. `rw [privacyFactor_eq s (by linarith), privacyFactor_eq t ht]`, then `rw [div_lt_div_iff₀ (by linarith) (by linarith)]`, then `nlinarith`.
