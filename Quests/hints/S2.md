## my_paradox
1. Make A the doctor who takes the hard cases: give A mostly large (hard) cases and B mostly small (easy) ones. A tiny, perfect A-small arm helps.
2. Test candidates with the #eval line until all three comparisons point the right way. Then `norm_num [Simpson, Arm.rate, Arm.pool, myASmall, myALarge, myBSmall, myBLarge]`.
3. A 9-patient example: A small 1/1, A large 1/3, B small 3/4, B large 0/1. Can you beat it?

## balanced_no_paradox
1. With the same denominator, comparing fractions is just comparing numerators.
2. `div_lt_div_iff_of_pos_right` says a/c < b/c ↔ a < b when 0 < c. Use `.mp` on h₁ and h₂ to get b₁ < a₁ and b₂ < a₂, then `.mpr` on the goal, then `linarith`.
3. `have h₁' : b₁ < a₁ := (div_lt_div_iff_of_pos_right hn₁).mp h₁` (same for h₂'), then `exact (div_lt_div_iff_of_pos_right (by linarith)).mpr (by linarith)`.
