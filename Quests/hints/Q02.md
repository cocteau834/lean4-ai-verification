# Hints · Q02 · The Tactic Toolkit

Spoilers! Use `python3 scripts/expedition.py hint <puzzle>` instead. It reveals one hint at a time and logs it for the Game Master.

## t1
1. A proof of `P → Q` is a function: feed it a proof of P and out comes a proof of Q.
2. `exact hPQ hP`

## t2
1. Take the "and" apart into its two pieces, then build a new "and" in the other order.
2. `obtain ⟨hp, hq⟩ := h` then `exact ⟨hq, hp⟩`

## t3
1. To prove "P or Q" you only need one side. Which one do you have?
2. `right` then `exact hQ`

## t4
1. You don't know which side of `h` is true, so handle both possibilities (proof by cases).
2. `rcases h with hp | hq`, then in the first case `right` and `exact hp`, in the second `left` and `exact hq`.

## t5
1. Assume P, then push it through both implications.
2. `intro hp` then `exact hQR (hPQ hp)`

## t6
1. `¬ X` means `X → False`. Assume P ∧ ¬P and play the two halves against each other.
2. `intro h` then `exact h.2 h.1` (h.1 : P, and h.2 : ¬P is a function from P to False).

## t7
1. Substitute: replace `a` using h1, then `b` using h2.
2. `rw [h1, h2]`

## t8
1. To prove "there exists n", name the n. What squares to 144?
2. `use 12`

## liar
1. Try both possibilities for A on paper, knight and knave. Both lead to nonsense.
2. Lean can try both too: `decide`.

## knightsB2
1. Suppose A were a knight. Then "we are both knaves" would be true, so A would be a knave. Contradiction!
2. So A is a knave and the statement is false: they are NOT both knaves. What does that make B?
3. `(false, true)`, then `decide`. (If `decide` ever says the proposition is false, your answer is wrong.)

## knightsB3
1. Suppose A is a knave. Then B is a knight, so B's claim "A and I are the same kind" is true... does that hold up?
2. A can't be a knave, so A is a knight and B is a knave. Check that B's statement really is false.
3. `(true, false)`, then `decide`.

## knightsB4
1. Start with A: suppose A is a knight and follow the chain of statements to C.
2. A as a knight forces C to be a knight who says something false. So A is a knave, which makes B a knight and C a knave.
3. `(false, true, false)`, then `decide`.
